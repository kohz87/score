### [high] Journal compaction removes the evidence needed to detect rollback

**Where:** [External journal](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/7.5-orchestrator.md:6), [v3.1 compaction](/home/raven/score/docs/reviews/2026-10-10-7.5-orchestrator-v3-lead.md:19).

**What happens:** A backup contains an unsent intent. Score subsequently dispatches and completes it, then compacts its journal entries because termination is recorded in the current database. Restoring the earlier database leaves neither its `sendStarted` marker nor an independent journal entry proving dispatch. Recovery can repeat the work once Hermes’s receipt is unavailable. Compaction can similarly erase evidence of a Stop that an older backup lacks.

**Why:** “Recorded in the DB” establishes reconciliation with today’s database; it does not protect against tomorrow’s restoration of an older one. The amendment specifies no retained journal checkpoint or compacted ledger. The journal also expressly lists Stop and global fences, without explicitly covering other admission-revoking transitions such as Finish and manual Pause.

**Change:** Retain an external monotonic checkpoint and sufficient compacted admission/fence tombstones to detect older databases. Journal every transition whose rollback can restore admission authority. Specify crash-safe compaction and reconciliation when detailed history has been compacted. Add database-only restore tests **after compaction**, and restore tests across Finish/Pause without another dispatch. The documented whole-home restore limitation can remain under the operator protocol.

### [medium] Requested approval can arrive after the assignment starts

**Where:** [`lead-asks`](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/7.5-orchestrator.md:40), [assignment admission](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/7.5-orchestrator.md:170).

**What happens:** The lead calls `orch.assign`, receives a step ID, then calls `orch.requestApproval(stepId)`. With capacity available, Score can admit the ungated step between those calls. The person is asked to approve work that has already begun.

**Why:** Serializing transactions does not make two tool calls atomic. Hermes serializes calls on its MCP connection, but Score’s scheduler can progress between them. Quoting injected results does not prevent the lead from making these valid calls in an unsafe order.

**Change:** Request approval **inside `assign`**, creating the frozen assignment and gate atomically. Alternatively, introduce a prepared assignment that is ineligible until explicitly committed. Reject attempts to add approval after admission begins. Define rejection as a committed disposition, including dependency blocking and its durable wake cause. Keep inquiries separate from authorization.

The default `every-step` policy remains the useful protection against injected results inducing valid assignments; `lead-asks` and `none` deliberately provide less protection.

### [medium] Capability recovery and redaction still need explicit protocols

**Where:** [Volatile capability insertion](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/7.5-orchestrator.md:42), [accepted Hermes history exposure](/home/raven/score/docs/reviews/2026-10-10-7.5-orchestrator-v3-lead.md:22), [D6](/home/raven/score/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:329).

**What happens:** After a crash, the instruction to mint a new generation “on re-dispatch” can be applied to a lead whose original POST may already have succeeded. Changing the capability changes the request payload: retaining the idempotency key can conflict, while changing it can create another lead. Separately, adding `orc_` only to Score’s server redactor can leave capabilities in bridge payloads or spools before server ingestion.

**Why:** Placeholder persistence solves plaintext intent storage, but cannot reconstruct the original volatile dispatch payload. Hermes [fingerprints the request body](/home/raven/.hermes/hermes-agent/gateway/platforms/api_server_runs.py:643). The inherited workflow rule [forbids uncertain admission replay](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/7.1-workflow-builder.md:90). The [bridge redactor](/home/raven/score/hermes-plugin/score_bridge/redact.py:27) is a separate boundary.

**Change:** Specify three recovery cases: never sent → mint and dispatch; known handle → observe the existing run without another POST; possibly sent without a handle → unknown, fenced, no replay. Define capability validity in each case. Require capability scrubbing in both redaction layers, before serialization/truncation, across streams, errors, receipts, and free-text fields. Scan complete step inputs before redaction/truncation. Accepting exposure in Hermes’s own transcript does not waive these Score obligations.

### [medium] Fresh lead sessions do not refresh Hermes’s MCP manifest

**Where:** [v3.1 amendment 4](/home/raven/score/docs/reviews/2026-10-10-7.5-orchestrator-v3-lead.md:28).

**What happens:** A running Hermes gateway discovered Score before `orch.*` existed. Score is upgraded, and a fresh orchestration session starts. The newly constructed agent can still receive the old MCP tool inventory.

**Why:** Hermes performs [gateway MCP discovery at startup](/home/raven/.hermes/hermes-agent/gateway/run.py:1908). Discovery [skips already-connected servers](/home/raven/.hermes/hermes-agent/tools/mcp_tool_discovery.py:359); building a fresh agent or refreshing its tools from the registry does not necessarily query the server again. [Model tool initialization explicitly avoids discovery](/home/raven/.hermes/hermes-agent/model_tools.py:151).

**Change:** Replace “always visible” with a tested reload/readiness protocol. Verify the required manifest in the Hermes process serving the lead profile; when unavailable, require an owner-controlled reload/restart before starting. A fresh session ID or a separate CLI inventory is insufficient proof. Test a long-lived gateway with an old manifest across a Score upgrade.

### [medium] “No open runs” does not prove complete, settled accounting

**Where:** [Accounting completeness](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/7.5-orchestrator.md:45), [budget rules](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/7.5-orchestrator.md:121).

**What happens:** A partial compression lineage has no known open runs and is labelled complete. Missing continuation costs disappear from the admission budget, or reservations are released before the relevant accounting is settled.

**Why:** The current [lineage reader](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/hermes/live/recover.ts:40) silently stops at ambiguity, errors, cycles, or twenty hops and returns only session IDs. Absence of open runs in that returned prefix cannot establish completeness. Auxiliary review usage also [lands outside the session cost row](/home/raven/.hermes/hermes-agent/agent/background_review.py:797).

**Change:** Define adapter facts for qualified ownership, lineage completeness and failure reasons, observation time, estimated/billed cost, cost scope, and settlement. Incomplete evidence remains unknown. Label excluded auxiliary usage explicitly. Specify how provisional spend and reservations reconcile without gaps or double counting. Test forks, missing continuations, traversal limits, and delayed final accounting.

### [medium] Ownership and abandonment still lack an executable transition contract

**Where:** [Ownership transitions](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/7.5-orchestrator.md:47), [deferred control matrix](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/7.5-orchestrator.md:125), [lead’s stage-B condition](/home/raven/score/docs/reviews/2026-10-10-7.5-orchestrator-v3-lead.md:15).

**What happens:** Existing controls can clear the wrong hold or admit ordinary work. Abandonment releases capacity and reservations, but does not determine whether Resume or replacement becomes eligible, whether unknown spend remains a budget hold, or precisely when ownership guards lift.

**Why:** `owned → draining → released` names states without supplying action behavior. Today, [Team Resume directly clears brief flags](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/http/team-pause.ts:96), [brief status derives from ordinary work](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/domain/briefs.ts:192), and [MCP room posts mutate rooms directly](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/mcp/tools.ts:123). Normal Hermes interruption can end permanently as `cancelled`, so administrative resolution is a routine path.

**Change:** Supply the table before implementing affected dispatch/control paths. Route Stop through the executor; make Resume release only its own hold; explicitly reject or delegate Retry, Start plan, summaries, room mutations, and MCP posts. Define lifecycle-to-brief/Now mappings and revision-bound human decisions. Abandonment must permanently retire admission authority, expire capabilities, ignore late results, retain owed stops/tombstones, and preserve unknown actual spend. Continuation alongside unresolved execution needs a separate recorded waiver.

### [medium] The reservation formula does not bound all retained work

**Where:** [Storage reserve and capacity](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/7.5-orchestrator.md:50), [prompt/command limits](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/7.5-orchestrator.md:133).

**What happens:** A permitted 64 KB frozen lead input consumes its entire nominal 64 KB allowance before results, notes, gates, receipts, transitions, and associated records. Reused room/run infrastructure can retain additional copies and observations outside the proposed calculation.

**Why:** Reserving a number does not define what it covers or constrain subsequent writes. The brief still lacks an accounting scope, bounded ongoing-write policy, explicit replay allowance, and exhaustion behavior for admitted obligations. The numerical ceilings also need shared atomic claims across the executor reused by 7.1 and 7.5.

**Change:** Define one covered-data inventory and compatible aggregate limits, accounting for rooms, messages, intents, receipts, transitions, associations, and replay—or cap replay separately. Reserve protected result/Stop space and specify which ordinary writes are refused or truncated when full. Define waiting-run limits and capacity accounting for both lead and step attempts. Test maximum-size snapshots plus command traffic, and quota exhaustion during result acceptance and Stop.

### [medium] Watch snapshot scope needs matching live-update and retention rules

**Where:** [Whole Watch response](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/7.5-orchestrator.md:53), [current Watch reducer](/home/raven/score/apps/web/src/routes/watch/watch-queries.ts:34), [independent cleanup](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/hub/retire.ts:96).

**What happens:** A correctly scoped snapshot is subsequently contaminated by another session’s live updates. Independently, seven-day cleanup removes evidence associated with an unresolved attempt while orchestration history remains protected.

**Why:** V3 closes the missing-response-fields requirement, but does not explicitly extend its predicate to live updates. Existing Watch subscriptions are agent-scoped, and existing frame cleanup does not consult orchestration obligations. A retention floor describes missing evidence; it does not define which unresolved evidence must remain protected.

**Change:** Apply one qualified attempt/lineage scope to snapshots, reducers, updates, pagination, and every derived field. Keep unassociated evidence explicitly unassociated. Define protected evidence, associated cleanup, and actual replay floors. Test concurrent sessions through snapshot and live updates, compression, and cleanup of unresolved attempts.

## The design I would approve

- Keep short event-driven lead runs, immediate-return tools, per-attempt bearer capabilities, explicit command IDs, and durable wake claims.
- Create requested gates atomically with assignments; bind paired decisions to immutable payloads and evidence.
- Preserve the prohibition on uncertain admission replay and distinguish canonical termination from transcripts, Stop acknowledgements, and human assertions.
- Retain rollback detection through journal compaction, covering every admission-revoking fence.
- Complete recovery, accounting, ownership, abandonment, quota, and live replay contracts before implementing their affected paths.
- Keep the acknowledgement covering unrestricted lead and step execution. Quoted results constrain parsing; validated tools constrain admitted authority. Neither establishes a prompt-injection sandbox or control over background work.
- Require fixture tests for the counterexamples above, including manifest refresh, post-compaction restore, late approval requests, and capability loss across crash boundaries.
