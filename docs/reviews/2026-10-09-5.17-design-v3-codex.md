### [high] History detection mistakes normal maintenance for restoration

**Where:** [V3 §1](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/5.17.md:28), Hermes [event GC](/home/raven/.hermes/hermes-agent/hermes_cli/kanban_db.py:4370) and [task deletion](/home/raven/.hermes/hermes-agent/hermes_cli/kanban_db.py:4003).

**What happens:** Normal GC can reduce `MAX(task_events.id)` below Score’s checkpoint while retaining every attempt. V3 then opens a false history and rematerializes those attempts under new `kr_` IDs. A sample containing legitimately deleted attempts can also fail after an inode change without proving replacement.

Conversely, an in-place restore can leave the immutable attempt tuple unchanged, restore a previously closed attempt to open, and accumulate enough new events to exceed the old checkpoint before Score polls. None of the listed rules detects that case.

**Why:** Retained-row maximum is not the allocation high-water mark. Missing rows are not necessarily contradictory rows. The disclaimer about absolute restore guarantees is appropriate, but detectable lifecycle regressions still need an ambiguity policy.

**Change:** Use validated allocation high-water evidence, such as `sqlite_sequence`, alongside retained boundary/claim evidence. Distinguish deletion, contradiction and insufficient evidence. Treat an observed terminal-to-open regression as a fault or ambiguous history requiring a gap. Add GC and restore-after-cursor-catch-up fixtures.

### [high] History qualification stops before task ownership and ongoing intervals

**Where:** [V3 §2](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/5.17.md:37), [brief task lookup](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/domain/briefs.ts:75), [room attachment](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/hermes/live/tasks.ts:65), [arrival holds](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/domain/briefs.ts:325).

**What happens:** `kanban_task_runs` qualifies run links, but existing `brief_tasks`, `task_rooms`, review edges and hold records still identify tasks by upstream ID alone. A replacement board reusing a task ID can inherit an unrelated room or brief—and an old paused brief can cause the new task to be parked.

An open run retained from a closed history also keeps `ended_at=NULL`. Current Now and Reports readers consequently continue its working interval indefinitely.

**Why:** Qualifying one relationship table does not qualify the joins consuming it. Closing a history ends reliable observation; it does not prove worker termination.

**Change:** Persist current source/history qualification in projection metadata and qualify or invalidate dependent associations and pending holds. Historical links must not resolve through replacement task projections. Define an observation-loss boundary for abandoned open runs, without fabricating an actual end time. Also define aggregate eligibility so copied ancestral attempts are not counted twice.

### [high] The classifier treats synthetic completion and handoff rows as proven claims

**Where:** [V3 §3](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/5.17.md:45), Hermes [synthetic writer](/home/raven/.hermes/hermes-agent/hermes_cli/kanban_db.py:2109), [completion](/home/raven/.hermes/hermes-agent/hermes_cli/kanban_db.py:2882), [review handoff](/home/raven/.hermes/hermes-agent/hermes_cli/kanban_db.py:3534) and [summary editing](/home/raven/.hermes/hermes-agent/hermes_cli/kanban_db.py:3263).

**What happens:** Hermes synthesizes never-claimed rows with outcomes `completed` and `review_requested`, including summaries and metadata. V3 explicitly accepts those outcomes as proof of a claim. Synthetic blocked and scheduled rows also carry the supplied reason as their summary. There is no field distinguishing a “worker summary” from operator-supplied prose.

The synthetic predicate also omits retained `claim_lock`, although the next predicate accepts an observed claim lock as proof. The predicates can therefore overlap.

**Why:** Outcomes and summaries describe transitions, not necessarily execution. Hermes’s ordinary claim writer retains the lock on closed attempts, so pruning a claim event does **not** automatically make its row partial.

**Change:** Give positive claim/process evidence precedence and persist it durably. Define classification against every supported writer, including legacy inflight backfill. Shared outcomes and prose must not establish a claim. Ambiguous rows stay explicitly partial; cleanup must not erase previously established proof. Fix the pruned-claim fixture to cover both retained-lock proof and genuinely missing provenance.

### [high] The runs rebuild fails with existing message links

**Where:** [V3 §4](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/5.17.md:54), [migration transaction](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/db/migrate.ts:87), [foreign-key configuration](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/db/database.ts:55), [incoming run reference](/home/raven/score/apps/server/migrations/0011_message_runs.sql:4).

**What happens:** A normal create/copy/drop/rename rebuild fails at `DROP TABLE runs` when a populated `message_runs.run_id` references it. Disabling foreign keys inside the existing transaction has no effect. `defer_foreign_keys` alone also failed the populated reproduction under the supplied Node runtime.

**Why:** “One transaction, data copied, indexes recreated” leaves out the incoming foreign-key procedure.

**Change:** Specify and test the exact algorithm. One successful in-memory approach staged the message/run links, temporarily cleared their nullable references, rebuilt `runs`, and restored the links within the same transaction.

Preserve every column, `STRICT`, primary/unique constraints, both explicit indexes—`runs_profile_status` and `runs_profile_session`—and all referencing data. There are no triggers on `runs` today; existing message/FTS triggers must remain intact. Check foreign keys before commit and test rollback and restart with populated active runs. Keeping old checksums untouched is correct.

### [high] Nullable attribution needs additional contract shapes

**Where:** [V3 §4](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/5.17.md:56), [Now lanes contract](/home/raven/score/packages/contract/src/http.ts:129), [Reports contract](/home/raven/score/packages/contract/src/http.ts:171), [lane filtering](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/domain/lanes.ts:250).

**What happens:** Making only `RunSummary.agentId` nullable does not express an Unattributed lane: Now requires a full `AgentSummary`, and the lane builder filters runs through the known-agent roster. Reports’ attribution fields require agent IDs. Its reader currently rejects null profiles before aggregation.

Flow’s run readers, profile-based selection, logs and Stop selection also assume non-null profiles. Notifications build a Watch URL directly from `run.agentId`.

**Why:** Updating parsers and labels does not define how these different response shapes carry unknown attribution.

**Change:** Specify the Now lane and Reports bucket representations explicitly, with no fabricated profile ID. Carry nullable attribution through Flow and logs, route unattributed notifications to a valid destination, and perform Kanban Stop rejection before requiring a profile. Preserve strict profile validation for gateway actions. Keep the unknown-attribution bucket separate from session-spend aggregation so it does not mechanically invalidate otherwise complete session accounting.

### [high] Revision identities do not bypass terminal projection guards

**Where:** [V3 §§4–5](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/5.17.md:58), [terminal guard](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/hub/projections.ts:126), [run lifecycle](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/hermes/live/translate.ts:22).

**What happens:** A higher revision still cannot update a terminal run through the default guarded upsert. Even `refreshTerminal:true` rejects `ended_unknown → completed`: it permits refresh only when the terminal state stays identical. This was reproduced in memory.

**Why:** Observation dedupe and projection lifecycle guards are separate mechanisms. The monotonic revision design fixes A→B→A identity reuse, but does not itself allow closed-row reconciliation.

**Change:** Define a Kanban projection operation that accepts newer same-terminal content and explicitly permitted unknown-terminal refinement while refusing reopening. Allocate revisions and compare hashes inside the writer transaction. Do not weaken gateway lifecycle guards globally. Qualify the A→B→A promise as three **observed snapshots**; polling cannot reconstruct every intermediate upstream edit.

### [high] Atomic pages need a defined dedupe and checkpoint model

**Where:** [V3 §§5–6](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/5.17.md:61), [ingest dedupe](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/hub/ingest.ts:172), [tailer reset](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/hermes/live/tailer.ts:80), [task revision guard](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/domain/tasks.ts:31).

**What happens:** §5 defines individual event and attempt observation identities; §6 submits one observation for the whole page. The wrapper identity and handling of the constituent identities are unspecified.

Ingest dedupes **before** `apply`. Reusing a page identity suppresses changed snapshots; attaching reconciliation to a retired event checkpoint suppresses it even with a fresh identity. Both behaviors were reproduced.

The existing tailer resets only on filesystem incarnation changes. A detected same-inode rollback also leaves the current task-projection revision guard rejecting lower event revisions unless its epoch changes.

**Why:** Transactional atomicity does not establish replay identity, snapshot freshness or epoch fencing.

**Change:** Define replayable page identities, per-event dedupe, and revision allocation within the single writer operation. Separate reconciliation from immutable event retirement floors. Fence checkpoints and task projections by logical history, not filesystem identity alone. Fold review transitions into the page transaction and advance in-memory backfill state only after successful commit. Serialize baseline, tail and sweep reads so an older snapshot cannot overwrite newer observations.

### [high] Numeric-link upgrade still uses incarnation as history proof

**Where:** [V3 §7](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/5.17.md:77), [legacy projection schema](/home/raven/score/apps/server/migrations/0010_tasks.sql:1), [numeric link construction](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/hermes/live/task-model.ts:160).

**What happens:** An old projection can retain the same filesystem incarnation across an in-place restore. Its numeric attempt ID may then identify unrelated current work. Conversely, an identical copy on a new inode can fail the proposed incarnation constraint.

Legacy projections contain numeric IDs and an incarnation, but no immutable attempt fingerprint establishing which claim those numbers meant.

**Why:** §1 correctly says filesystem identity is observation, not proof; §7 still relies on it to authorize association.

**Change:** Define a matching predicate using all retained independent provenance, including task identity and any saved claim/attempt evidence. Same-incarnation equality alone must not authorize mapping. Preserve genuinely ambiguous links as unresolved.

Specify their storage and API representation: `TaskDetail.runIds` currently contains only strings and has no unresolved-link field. Snapshot upgrade inputs before normal reconciliation replaces them, and refresh persisted idempotency receipts as well as ordinary cached details.

### [high] Requested changes can still become a passed review

**Where:** [V3 §8](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/5.17.md:83), [Now review checks](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/domain/now.ts:218), [current tie-breaker](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/domain/briefs.ts:101).

**What happens:** Pre2 still infers a passed review from completed execution, current task completion and absence of later implementation work by timestamp. When rejection, retry and final approval share a second, the strict `>` comparison misses the retry. The reproduction returned `ok:true` for both reviewer attempts.

Opaque UUIDs also invalidate existing chronological tie-breakers that sort by run ID. Now still derives review role from current assignee/reviewer fields.

**Why:** Execution completion and current task state cannot establish an earlier attempt’s verdict.

**Change:** Generate checks from exact persisted outcome/verdict evidence. Use stored claim role and numeric upstream attempt ordering throughout Now, Flow and history selection. Unknown verdicts must remain unknown. Add a combined same-second rejection/retry/approval fixture, including reassignment and null-role cases.

### [medium] Unknown tools and attempt-specific Watch remain incompatible with consumers

**Where:** [V3 scope](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/5.17.md:7), [tools contract](/home/raven/score/packages/contract/src/types.ts:316), [Flow tools contract](/home/raven/score/packages/contract/src/briefs.ts:72), [Watch lookup](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/domain/brief-flow.ts:547).

**What happens:** V3 promises unknown tools as `null`, but storage and contracts use required arrays, and Flow falls back to `[]`. Flow also displays the latest Watch surface for the agent, regardless of whether it belongs to the selected Kanban attempt.

**Why:** Absent observation is not an established empty tool inventory. Profile-wide Watch activity does not establish attempt provenance.

**Change:** Choose explicit nullable or completeness-aware tool fields through storage, contracts and UI. Keep attempt replay links unknown in 5.17a-1; any general agent Watch link must be labelled as general activity.

### [medium] Baseline, sweeps and acceptance fixtures need stronger recovery oracles

**Where:** [V3 sweep/baseline policy](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/5.17.md:67), [acceptance fixtures](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/5.17.md:100), [current whole-board read](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/hermes/live/tasks.ts:41).

**What happens:** The paged cursor is a useful improvement, but the fixtures do not establish bounded, resumable integration with the current reader, which loads the whole board and every task’s attempts. “Closed attempts changed in the last 24 h” also has no upstream query field: `task_runs` has neither revision nor `updated_at`.

Several fixture expectations are too broad: pruned claims need not be partial, and not every restore is detectable.

**Why:** A named scenario without precise inputs, crash boundaries and expected durable state can pass while recovery remains incomplete.

**Change:** Specify the recent-closed selection predicate and bounded source queries. Test multiple pages, restart between pages, source replacement mid-baseline, old closed-row cleanup, reconciliation without new events, and upgrade while Hermes is unavailable. Inject failures inside the writer transaction and after commit. Add populated migration, GC, redaction/byte-limit and combined consumer fixtures. Interleave polls in the three-revision test.

### [medium] Bridge deferral is safe, but its requirements omit necessary obligations

**Where:** [5.17a-2 requirements](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/5.17.md:108).

**What happens:** The outline addresses producer versus execution identity, host isolation, compression, observed models, tool-observation identity and normalization. It does not explicitly require:

- A distinct, idempotent association operation after original ingest, atomic with pending resolution and Watch updates.
- Terminal enrichment and field ownership preventing Kanban snapshots from erasing bridge-owned data.
- Profile/source-qualified session keys and updates to Flow logs and Now file consumers.
- Session-accounting completeness/window rules for any future attempt cost attribution.

**Why:** These were independent v2 recovery and consumer findings; relinking Watch frames alone does not resolve them.

**Change:** Add those requirements and their acceptance cases to the future review gate. In 5.17a-1, durably retain the claim lock, claim-event/source-status evidence and observed process provenance needed for later validation; the listed mapping fields do not currently specify that evidence.

## The design I would approve

Keep the split, opaque run IDs, monotonic revisions, explicit unknowns, session-only spend and Stop unavailability. Add the following before implementation approval:

- A maintenance-aware history policy covering ambiguity, abandoned open observations and qualified task/brief ownership.
- A writer-derived classifier with durable, persistent claim evidence and explicit partial attribution.
- A tested foreign-key-safe rebuild preserving populated Score data and schema objects.
- Concrete nullable/completeness-aware contracts and exact verdict, role and ordering rules.
- One history-fenced ingestion coordinator with defined page identities, transactional revisions and separately scoped reconciliation.
- Conservative, resumable legacy-link upgrade and bounded baseline/sweep work, verified across crash boundaries.

5.17a-2 can remain deferred once its missing requirements are recorded. Stop should remain unavailable until the separate upstream operation and its recovery semantics pass review.
