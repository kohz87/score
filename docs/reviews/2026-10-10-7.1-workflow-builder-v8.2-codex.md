### [high] Recovery leaves an unsent consumer authorized to dispatch withdrawn evidence

**Where:** [Recovery repair](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/domain/orchestration/executor.ts:822), [send checks](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/domain/orchestration/executor.ts:1311), [fix-I regression](/home/raven/score/apps/server/test/orchestration/fix-i.test.ts:182).

**What happens:** Before upgrade, A’s synthetic recovered success has already supplied B’s frozen input. B is `admitted`, but its external attempt remains `created`—a normal crash boundary between claim and send. Recovery clears A’s result and makes A unknown, yet leaves B’s input and approved gate intact. Abandon A → Resume removes the temporary unknown fence, and B sends the withdrawn text.

A memory-only probe confirmed: after repair, B remained `admitted` with its gate `approved`; after Abandon → Resume, `beginSend` authorized B and changed its attempt to `send_started`.

**Why:** Repair withdraws consumers only when their step is `queued` or `pending_gate`. The dependency eligibility check occurs during claim; the send barrier does not repeat it for an existing attempt. The regression manually sets B to `queued` and never creates B’s external attempt, so it misses this boundary.

**Change:** Retire/refuse every provably unsent consuming attempt, invalidate its approval and frozen input, and verify result eligibility at send. Record already-sent consumers separately. Apply equivalent invalidation through `wf_consumed`, because workflow assignments deliberately omit `after`. Add an upgrade → repair → Abandon → Resume test containing an actual `created` consumer attempt.

### [medium] Dispatch generations lack a bounded, generation-preserving compaction protocol

**Where:** [V8.2 dispatch generations](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/7.1-workflow-builder.md:7), [storage inventory](/home/raven/score/docs/briefs/7.1-workflow-builder.md:57), [current compaction](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/domain/orchestration/executor.ts:1766), [tombstone construction](/home/raven/score/apps/server/src/domain/orchestration/journal.ts:305).

**What happens:** After five certified refusals, a deadline-free run can generate another journaled send/refusal pair every ten minutes indefinitely. Reusing one attempt bounds attempt rows, but does not bound its durable proof records. Returning that attempt to `created` also prevents today’s state-based compactor from recognizing its earlier generations as reconciled.

When compaction eventually occurs, current send tombstones discard `generation`; the protocol must define how those tombstones preserve the new restore checks.

**Why:** Backoff bounds retry frequency, not lifetime storage. V8.2 specifies qualified send/refusal checks but leaves their interaction with permanent tombstones and compaction unspecified. The journal’s size alarm supplies notification rather than a storage bound.

**Change:** Define a bounded witness for refused generations that preserves the latest qualified send/refusal state and monotonic restore evidence, or impose a lifetime refusal limit with explicit human continuation. Update compaction, tombstones, recovery and fence lifting together. Test prolonged refusal churn and restoration after compaction across both a refused generation and a later accepted generation.
