# 7.5 Orchestrator — design v2 (2026-10-10)

v1 review (`docs/reviews/2026-10-10-7.5-orchestrator-codex.md`): not approved (7 high, 6 medium); the reviewer endorses
short event-driven lead runs with immediate-return tools. v2 keeps that and adds the missing protocols. Where v2 and v1
differ, v2 wins.

## Authority: a per-turn capability
- Each **lead attempt** gets a fresh unguessable capability `orc_<random 32 B>` (hashed at rest) bound to (orchestration,
  lead attempt, turn generation, lead agent incarnation), included **only** in that attempt's rendered input. Every
  `orch.*` tool requires it as an argument; the MCP agent token must also match the lead. A capability expires when its
  attempt is terminated, retired, or superseded; calls with an older generation are rejected.
- It is bearer authority: redacted everywhere (`orc_` added to `redact()` like `scm_`), never forwarded to steps (step
  inputs are built separately and scanned for the prefix; a match blocks admission), never logged.

## Typed dispatch for lead and steps
Both lead attempts and steps go through an orchestration-only dispatch path with a fixed recipient: no mention routing,
no plan materialization, no ordinary room-context assembly, no automatic summaries (7.4a off for orchestration rooms).
Each lead attempt and each step attempt has its own session (`orc-<run>-lead-<n>`, `orc-<run>-<step>-<attempt>`). The
lead's input is frozen at admission (goal, bounded plan, step table, consumed result revisions, gates, budget/limits).

## Acknowledged unrestricted execution (today)
Starting an orchestration requires the person's acknowledgement covering **both** the lead and the steps: "Score
controls the work it starts (turns and steps) and can stop those runs; agents may still use other tools and start
background work Score can't see or stop." Controls and accounting are described as covering observed, Score-started
work only. A future per-run tool policy (proposal) narrows this but is not, alone, proof of quiescence.

## Gates bound to assignments
`gatePolicy: 'every-step' | 'lead-asks' | 'none'`. With `every-step` (default), `orch.assign` atomically creates the
assignment **and** its gate, freezing agent + incarnation, instruction, dependencies, estimate and the evidence shown;
one paired, revision-bound decision approves exactly that payload. `orch.ask` creates an **inquiry** whose answer is
data for the next lead turn and never changes permissions. With `none`, assignments are admitted without a gate; budget
uncertainty and runtime approvals (Hermes tool approvals) still go to the person as before. Gate, budget, manual,
agent, team holds are independent.

## Durable commands, wake-ups and recovery
- Every `orch.*` call is a command with an id (MCP request id + capability generation): command identity, payload hash,
  the mutation, reservations and the returned receipt commit in **one** transaction; a repeated call returns the stored
  receipt. Calls arriving while their lead attempt's admission response isn't recorded yet are rejected (`retry`).
- Step results are accepted and the orchestration's **wake cause** recorded in the same transaction. One lead attempt is
  claimed against a durable cause cursor (`causes_consumed_up_to`) with its frozen input; a crash before or after the
  claim re-derives the same input from the cursor; at most one lead attempt runs.
- Recovery of lead and step intents is **only** through the orchestration executor (owner `orchestration`); generic
  intent recovery skips them; startup order as in 7.1 v3.

## Restore: a global admission fence
The outside epoch witness (7.1 v6) is checked at every start. A mismatch, a missing/unreadable witness, or a database
older than the witness puts a **persistent global admission fence** on all orchestrations and workflows (even if no run
looks unfinished — restored history may have lost them). Only the person lifts it, after a reconciliation screen that
lists the witness counter gap and every run/attempt whose state may be missing; lifting it is a recorded decision, never
termination evidence. Assumption documented: the witness file survives a database-only restore; a whole-home restore
also rewinds the witness, so the reconciliation screen is shown on every start after the home's identity
(`$SCORE_HOME/instance-id` + boot-time machine id) changes.

## Finish, cancel, unknown, abandonment
Per attempt: `retired`, `stopRequested`, `stopAcknowledged`, `terminated` (evidence as 7.1 v6) are separate. `orch.finish`
is a durable **finish request**: it seals further commands; the run ends `succeeded` only when the requesting lead attempt
and every admitted attempt are terminated and no gate/inquiry is open; otherwise it drains (owed stops, waiting) and
ends `finished-with-unresolved` showing what is unconfirmed. `orch.cancel(step)` retires the step (no admission), stops
it if admitted, and requires termination before any replacement step for the same purpose counts. Unknown execution
fences the run (no new admissions) until resolved. **Abandonment** is a separate human action: it releases capacity and
reservations for listed unresolved attempts, keeps their owed stops, and records that late results are ignored.

## Mediums
- **Budget:** owner-set estimates per lead turn and per step (defaults in Settings); reserved once at queueing,
  transferred through admission, reconciled at admission with fresh per-attempt spend; auxiliary usage (summaries,
  title generation) outside the observed scope is labelled; unknown stays unknown; settlement only after termination and
  lineage closure.
- **Controls matrix:** in the contract before building — every room, message, summary, MCP, Flow, brief, team and agent
  action on orchestration-owned objects is delegated to the executor or rejected `409 orchestration_owned` while
  obligations remain; typed Now decisions (gate, inquiry, budget, reconciliation) with expected revisions, snapshot
  watermarks, events, durable history.
- **Dependencies and dispositions:** `after` ids must be existing earlier steps of the same run; a failed/cancelled
  dependency marks dependents `blocked` and wakes the lead to decide (cancel, replace); when turns are exhausted or no
  wake source remains, the run pauses with "needs you" (no extra lead turn spent); budget/deadline handling never needs
  a lead turn.
- **Limits:** every field bounded (goal 8 KB, instruction 8 KB, plan ≤ 30 entries × 500 B, notes 2 KB, commands per lead
  attempt ≤ 20, rendered lead input ≤ 64 KB with deterministic result selection: newest first, truncated with markers);
  capacity counts admitting, stopping, paused-active and unknown work; storage reserved for obligations and Stop records;
  pruning only after a persisted quiescence/abandonment boundary, keeping tombstones.
- **Watch:** attempt/session ownership persisted before dispatch, compression continuations included, uncertain
  associations shown as such, retention floors exposed.
- **Local mode:** v1 requires remote mode (the MCP server lives on the loopback bridge listener); the start screen
  preflights listener, the lead's MCP access with writes, and tool readiness, and explains what's missing. A local-mode
  loopback listener is a later item.

## Tests (added)
Cross-session and stale-generation capability calls, delayed and duplicated commands, lost command replies, wake-up
crashes around the claim, competing gate decisions, cancel/replacement overlap, whole-home restore and witness loss,
lost Stop fences, quota exhaustion, capability redaction and step-input scanning.

# Design v1 (2026-10-10, not approved)

**Goal (owner):** an agentic workflow: the person gives a goal; a lead agent decides the steps at runtime, hands them to
team agents, asks the person when needed, and finishes — instead of a drawn graph (7.1 comes later and reuses this
executor). Score is the referee: every request the lead makes is validated against the run's limits, budget, gates and
permissions.

## Shape: event-driven lead turns (no blocking tools)
- An **orchestration** is a Score-owned run: `{ goal, lead, allowedAgents, budgetUsd?, deadline?, maxSteps (≤ 30),
  maxLeadTurns (≤ 40), maxConcurrentSteps (≤ 3), gatePolicy }`, its own room and brief (owner: `orchestration`), created
  by the person (paired). It starts with a **lead turn**.
- A **lead turn** is a normal Hermes room run of the lead with a structured input: the goal, the plan so far, every step
  with its status and (quoted, sanitized, ≤ 8 KB each) result, pending gates, remaining budget/limits, and the allowed
  agents. The lead answers by calling orchestrator tools (below) and ends its turn. It never waits inside a turn.
- **Score wakes the lead** with the next lead turn when something it asked for resolves: a step finishes or becomes
  unknown, a gate is decided, a deadline/budget threshold is hit — batched (at most one lead turn running; events that
  arrive meanwhile are delivered in the next turn).
- Steps are ordinary Score-started room runs of the target agent (fixed recipient, routing and hand-offs disabled,
  per-attempt session `orch-<run>-<step>-<attempt>`), so Now/Flow/Reports/Watch show them; results are text (7.1 rules).

## Orchestrator tools (MCP, 7.4d server)
`orch.plan(steps[])` (record/replace the visible plan; no execution), `orch.assign(agent, instruction, after?: stepIds)`
→ `stepId` (queued; Score admits it when its `after` steps succeeded and limits allow), `orch.cancel(stepId)`,
`orch.ask(question)` → gate (shown in Now's Needs you; the person answers), `orch.note(text)` (progress line in the
room), `orch.finish(outcome, summary)`. No tool approves anything, reads other agents' sessions, changes settings,
or starts work outside the run.
- **Identity and scope:** the lead's MCP token identifies the agent; a call is accepted only if that agent is the lead
  of an orchestration with a **running lead turn**, and it applies to that orchestration. (Open question for review: the
  same agent busy in another session at the same time — v1 refuses to start an orchestration while its lead has any
  other active run, and holds the lead's other rooms' turns while a lead turn runs.)
- **Validation per call:** agent ∈ allowedAgents and served; workflow eligibility (7.1 v6 tool-policy rule: without a
  per-run tool policy the run needs the person's acknowledgement); instruction ≤ 8 KB, redacted; step and turn limits;
  budget reservation (node estimate or a default) against remaining budget; deadline not passed; run not stopping/
  paused/quarantined. Refusals are returned to the lead as tool errors and shown in the room.

## Safety (from the 7.1 reviews)
- Durable intents owned by the orchestration; one admission authority; `sendStarted` before each POST; `not_dispatched`
  only without `sendStarted`; restore quarantine via the outside epoch counter.
- Termination evidence = Hermes `completed`/`failed` from the agent loop; anything else `unknown` with obligations kept.
- Stop: run fence → no new lead turns or steps; owed stops; "not confirmed ended" shown until evidence.
- Budgets: soft, checked at each admission (lead turns included) with fresh per-attempt spend; reservations; unknown
  spend pauses by default. Deadlines absolute; expiry fences.
- Prompt injection: step results and person answers are quoted data inside the lead's input; the lead's tool calls are
  the only way it acts, and each is validated; a step can't call orchestrator tools (only the lead's token during its
  turn can); gates are human decisions only.
- Limits: as above, plus total stored data per run ≤ 2 MB; retention 90 days for finished runs.

## Product
"New orchestration" (Chat or Flow): goal, lead, allowed agents, optional budget/deadline, gate policy ("ask me before
each step" | "ask only when the lead asks" | "never ask"), the background-work acknowledgement. The room shows the plan,
step cards (agent, status, result), gates inline, notes; Stop / Pause / Resume. Flow shows the run as a live graph from
the recorded steps and `after` edges.

## Out of scope (v1)
Nested orchestrations, file artifacts between steps (text only), Kanban-task steps, coding agents as the lead (they can
be step agents once 7.2's capability matrix says so), editing the plan by hand mid-run (Stop and restart instead).

## Tests
Fake adapter + recorded fixtures, no model calls: the event loop (assign → step → wake → finish), batching of events
into one lead turn, every refusal path, limits, budget reservations and pauses, gates (each policy), Stop during admission
and with late admission, unknown steps, restart recovery and restore quarantine, injection attempts in step results
(text that looks like tool calls stays data), a lead calling tools outside its turn (refused).
